# Mayhem Companion

[Project website](https://mrmegamango.github.io/mayhem-companion/) · [Setup](#start) · [Feedback](#feedback)

A personal Windows companion for ARAM Mayhem, Arena and TFT. ARAM mode reads your current League champion and visible augment titles, then shows champion-specific augment, item-build and spell-pair win rates from ARAMKit. Arena uses its own champion, augment and individual-item statistics. TFT mode provides composition statistics, unit-specific three-item builds, and augment reference/expert tiers.

## Start

Run `Setup.ps1` with PowerShell on Windows (Python 3.12+ required). If Windows treats a WSL/network path as an unsigned remote script, copy the project to a local Windows folder before running setup. Open **Mayhem Companion** from the desktop shortcut. Installed files live in `%USERPROFILE%\MayhemCompanion`; the development source can remain in WSL.

The companion starts hidden and waits in the background. It appears automatically when you enter **ARAM, ARAM Mayhem or Arena champion select**, or when **TFT starts**, stays available through loading and the match, and hides when the match ends or you leave champion select. Other games and the ordinary lobby do not open it. Custom games are recognized by their actual mode. **Ctrl+Alt+M** opens or hides it manually; hiding it during a match lasts until that match ends. Start with `--browse` to keep manual browsing available outside matches. Closing with × exits the background companion.

The header's **Auto / ARAM / Arena / TFT** selector follows the detected game by default. Choose a mode to browse its reference manually. All modes use the same tray, League startup watcher and preferred portrait monitor.

The **M icon in the Windows notification area** remains available while the panel is hidden. Click it to show the panel, or right-click for **Show companion**, **Hide companion**, **Send feedback**, and **Exit**. Showing from the tray also unlocks click-through controls. Windows may place the icon in its hidden-icons menu. Exit removes the icon and stops the background companion.

Turn on **Start with League** in the tray menu to launch automatically when the League client opens. This installs one shortcut in your own Windows Startup folder: a small watcher starts at sign-in, checks League process names every two seconds, and launches the companion once per League session. Exit stays effective for that session; closing and reopening League starts it again. The watcher also counts an active game so a client closing during a match does not trigger another launch. Turn the tray setting off to remove the startup shortcut and stop the watcher. Setup also accepts `-StartWithLeague`. Outside the app, run `cscript //NoLogo Configure-Autostart.vbs "%USERPROFILE%\MayhemCompanion" disable` from the installed folder to disable it.

- Use **borderless or windowed** League. Exclusive fullscreen is not supported.
- Keep **Auto scan** checked. **Auto** champion selection follows your champion during champion select and in game, including champion swaps. Manually choosing a champion also works with automatic augment scanning.
- Keep League focused with the three cards visible while scanning. Switching away pauses the reader and retains the last recognized choices with a paused label. Returning to League resumes scanning; choices clear after the selection disappears or the game ends.
- Scanning captures and reads only the card-title band first at up to 4K resolution; the wider game area is captured only when that band contains no readable text. An unreadable capture retains the last results with an explicit retry message. Each scan leaves a pause after OCR finishes, including when recognition is slow.
- To check whether scanning affects game FPS, uncheck **Auto scan** while keeping the companion open. Manual data lookup remains available. Hiding the panel alone does not stop scanning; **Exit** stops the companion completely.
- Move the panel by dragging **MAYHEM**. Keep it away from the three augment cards.
- Placement uses the chosen monitor's work area, including displays left of or above the main screen. The saved display preference is preserved across resizing and restarts, with a visible fallback if that monitor is disconnected. The visible panel checks its actual position after opening and periodically afterward, returning to the preferred monitor if Windows relocates it or the monitor reconnects. Temporary monitor-query failures retain the last known display; dragging within the preferred screen remains available.
- **Ctrl+Alt+M** shows/hides the panel. **Ctrl+Alt+L** toggles click-through. **Ctrl+Alt+S** resumes/rescans.
- If OCR misses a choice, type its name in a dropdown and press Enter. Choose a champion manually to browse outside a match. **Auto** returns to automatic detection.
- **Select** opens automatically during ARAM champion select. It lists your current champion, initial champion-choice cards while available, and eligible bench champions. Champion portraits, overall Mayhem win rates and game counts are sorted by win rate; your current champion is marked with a dot. Champions outside your eligible choices or held by teammates are excluded. Brief client-side swap animation cooldowns are not modeled. The list updates as choices change and disappears when the match begins. If some local choice data is unavailable, confirmed choices remain with a **Some choices unavailable** note; missing rates display a dash.
- Spell-pair rates stay below the champion choices in **Select**. The tab returns to **Spells** outside champion select. It follows your champion and highlights your current pair as you change spells. A hovered champion is labeled as a preview. The statistics describe pairs (for example, Flash + Mark), not individual spells. The companion does not select a champion or change your spells.
- **Builds** opens when the game starts and between augment selections. It shows up to five core item sets for the selected build type, with win rates, game counts and pick rates within that type. The default sort is **Win rate**, highest first; choose **Games** or **Pick rate** to compare other sets. This also applies to follow-up items. Sets use ARAMKit's filtered build data and do not imply a purchase order. **Augments** opens when choices are recognized and **Builds** returns after repeated successful scans confirm they disappeared. Rerolls, focus loss and capture errors do not bounce between tabs. You can switch tabs manually; that choice stays until the next selection or game phase. Manual champion browsing keeps its selected tab.
- **All picks** shows the champion's overall augment statistics. Pick 1–4 filters are manual because level alone cannot reliably identify pending selections or extra-augment mechanics. Additional picks use All picks.
- After **three completed non-boot items**, Builds switches to follow-up item win rates for a core set matching your inventory. Components and boots do not trigger the switch. Already-owned items are removed as you buy them. If several published cores match, choose one in the build dropdown. The source conditions follow-up rates on the first three items' **purchase order**: the most-played order is selected initially and shown above the results, and you can select another order. Inventory slot order is never treated as purchase order. These are individual follow-up item rates, not an exact six-item combination's win rate; no combined rate is fabricated. Missing matches stay explicitly unavailable.
- English augment titles are supported. Native Windows English OCR must be installed. OCR never chooses an augment or presses an in-game button.

## Feedback

Choose **Send feedback** from the tray menu or at the top of the compact **?** help window. Keyboard shortcuts stay visible there; **Full guide** opens this README and **About & policies** expands a scrollable details panel. Select **Bug**, **Suggestion**, or **Incorrect stats** and describe what happened; the report includes the app version and selected game mode. A reply address and technical details are optional. You can review the report before sharing it. Technical details use a limited set of app/environment fields, not raw logs or League credentials.

**Open email draft** prepares a message to **amangousa@gmail.com** in your email app. Review it and press Send there; opening a draft does not submit feedback. You can attach a screenshot yourself in the email app. If no email app is configured, choose **Copy report** and paste it into your preferred email service. No GitHub account is required for email feedback.

You can also use the [GitHub issue forms](https://github.com/MrMegaMango/mayhem-companion/issues/new/choose) for bug reports, suggestions, and incorrect statistics. GitHub submissions require signing in and are public; do not include credentials or personal information in them.

## Data

Arena data comes from [winrate.lol's Arena pages](https://winrate.lol/en/arena). **Champions** shows all champions in that source's catalog, with observed rates where available; it does not claim that every row is selectable in your lobby. Your current champion is marked, and double-clicking a row opens manual browsing. **Items** shows champion-specific individual-item outcomes with icons, sorted by win rate. **Augments** displays recognized cards left to right during play, returning to Items once the cards disappear. Outside a choice screen, the Augments list can be searched manually. These do not use Mayhem rates, spell-pair statistics, exact six-item builds or inferred purchase routes.

Arena preserves the source's **Win rate** label: its finish cutoff is not specified, so it is not relabeled as top-four or first-place rate. First-place rates appear separately only where published (currently champion rankings); average placement is available in row details. The source does not separate augment picks or upgrade levels. Rank/region are unspecified and shown as such; the patch and cached state are displayed, with fetch time and metric notes behind **About rates**. Samples below 200 are marked. Current Arena coverage is sparse, and some champion/augment pairs have no observations; those stay blank instead of using global augment rates. Bravery, 3×6 and PBE queues are identified as game variants, while statistics retain the source's general Arena scope.

Arena pages are loaded on demand on a separate background thread and cached for an hour. Transient failures may use a labeled cached page with its original patch; denied or structurally invalid pages do not silently fall back. Champion identities come from Riot Data Dragon. OCR names are supplemented by a names-only Riot game-data snapshot (`mayhem/arena_augments.json`, version 16.21.1), including explicit CHERRY imports. Those reference names contain no statistics and do not assert current champion eligibility. New or changed augment names may require a catalog update. Artwork is fetched only for visible rows.

Data source: [ARAMKit](https://aramkit.com/en-US), using its public version manifest, champion/augment catalogs and the currently selected champion's stats. The app downloads catalog data, requested champions and icons for displayed items, caches immutable snapshots, and checks the manifest at most daily. No key or account is needed.

Available-champion rates use ARAMKit's single aggregate champion-ranking snapshot, with the source's **All brackets** sample. These are **ARAM Mayhem** statistics, including when browsing them from ordinary ARAM selection; they are not relabeled as ordinary ARAM rates. Counts are appearances of that champion in the source's match sample. The selected choices are compared locally against the cached dataset. Changing choices does not download each champion's full detail file. Rankings preload at startup and when matchmaking/ready check begins. Catalog, spell, build and augment downloads run separately from local polling and OCR, so slow public data cannot delay detecting the choice list. The view shows its own patch/date and labels a coherent cached fallback when needed.

TFT composition and item-build statistics come from [Tactics.tools](https://tactics.tools/team-compositions). **Comps** shows archetype outcomes and a common board variation, with unit icons, average placement, top-four rate, first-place rate, and games. **Item builds** loads exact three-item sets for the selected unit on demand and defaults to highest **1st place** rate; those rates are not the unit's overall rate. The displayed sample is **Ranked, Diamond+** and is explicitly labeled even if you are playing another TFT queue. Source patch, set and dataset update time are shown. Comps are manually browsed; this version does not read your board, shop or opponents.

TFT **Augments** provides descriptions and, when the current set matches, [MetaTFT's expert tier list](https://www.metatft.com/augments). Use **Compare 3** to enter the choices you see. Expert tiers are not augment win rates, and unrated augments remain reference-only. TFT augment choices are entered manually in this version. TFT datasets are cached for an hour, refreshes run separately from local polling/OCR, and unavailable data uses a labeled cached snapshot when a valid fallback exists. Unit builds and artwork are fetched only as needed.

Build cards show each item's icon beside its name. Icons come from the matching ARAMKit item catalog, download on a separate background thread, and are cached locally. Missing or unavailable icons leave the item names and statistics usable and do not delay augment scanning.

Rates are **champion + augment**, **champion + core item set**, or **champion + spell pair**, never global rates substituted for missing champion data. Missing data is blank; unavailable builds and pairs are not invented. Samples below 200 games are marked (an asterisk in the Spells table). Cached fallback data retains its original patch/date and is labeled. The rates reflect ARAMKit's sample, not all regions; collection/provenance claims belong to that provider. Source attribution is clickable in the panel. Respect [ARAMKit's terms](https://aramkit.com/en-US/terms); this is for personal, noncommercial use, not bulk redistribution of its data.

Riot's [developer policy](https://developer.riotgames.com/docs/lol#game-policy) lists augment win-rate displays in websites, applications and overlays as an unapproved use case. The wording refers to augments broadly without specifically naming Mayhem. This personal prototype is not Riot-approved; applying that restriction to Mayhem is a reasonable reading of the text, not a separate Mayhem-specific ruling verified by this project. No claim is made about enforcement, account bans, or guaranteed account safety.

## Local operation

Champion select is read from the local League Client API, using the current client's lockfile for its temporary local authentication token and port. The reader only issues GET requests for gameflow phase, gameflow session (queue/mode), and champion-select session, and matches your local player slot. Polls leave a 0.25-second pause during matchmaking, ready check and champion select, and 0.75 seconds otherwise. Known/default installation paths are tried before process discovery. During ARAM selection it also reads the small subset-choice and pickable-ID lists when needed to determine assigned cards and eligible bench champions. Initial cards can appear in the planning phase before your pick action becomes active; they are never predicted before the client provides them. It never displays the whole owned/pickable roster as the choice list. The token stays in memory and is never logged, cached, or uploaded. Riot does not guarantee compatibility of League Client API endpoints; manual champion browsing remains available if the client changes.

In-game champion and inventory detection use Riot's documented read-only Live Client Data API on Windows `127.0.0.1:2999`. Item IDs come from your matched active-player record in the existing player-list request; inventory is matched locally and is not uploaded. TLS relaxation is scoped to these loopback services, and redirects and proxies are refused. Public stats use normal HTTPS verification. The app does not read game memory, inject code, modify item sets, install drivers, automate gameplay, or request your account password.

Screen capture runs only when the League game executable is the visible foreground window. Screenshots remain in memory and OCR runs locally. No screenshots, player names, Riot IDs or game histories are uploaded automatically. Feedback includes only the report you choose to share; screenshots can be attached manually in your email app or on GitHub. HTTP requests to ARAMKit identify the public champion ID being looked up, as a normal page lookup would. Cache, window position and error logs are under `%USERPROFILE%\MayhemCompanion`.

Capture copies the requested rectangle directly through Windows GDI instead of reading both monitors and cropping afterward. The usual title band is 8.75% of the game frame (9.5% for Arena's slightly wider title row); the broader layout fallback is captured only if needed. OCR leaves a 0.35-second pause after an active scan completes, 0.8 seconds between idle/paused scans, and 10 seconds after an OCR error. Disabling Auto scan stops capture/OCR while retaining manual lookups and local game polling.

The app registers modified hotkeys without keyboard hooks. Automatic launch is a per-user Startup shortcut and needs no administrator access. Close with ×. To uninstall, disable **Start with League**, close the companion, and delete its installed folder and desktop shortcut.

## Development and verification

From the repository, using Windows Python:

```powershell
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python -m unittest discover -s tests -v
python -m mayhem --browse
```

Python under WSL cannot use the Windows OCR API or reach League's Windows loopback API by default. Use Windows Python for the app; pure unit tests can run in either environment.

Checks cover source validation/caching, champion matching, wrong-player rejection, spell-pair validation/current-pair matching, stale-response handling, OCR title ambiguity and layout, focus-pause retention, and failure states. Native English OCR was tested using synthetic title images and a live custom Mayhem game at 3840×2160: all three choices populated their champion-specific rates, including a hovered tooltip repeating a card title. Keeping those results while switching focus was also verified. Spell detection was verified against a live game's local API. OCR may still miss stylized/wrapped titles or unusual display layouts; use the manual dropdown fallback.

The direct-region capture update was checked with a temporary native title fixture on the portrait monitor: pixel colors/orientation, all three recognized titles, and stable GDI handle counts after repeated captures. A 2688×270 synthetic title band also retained all three titles. In a 20-sample desktop fixture comparison, median capture time fell from 115.5 ms for Pillow's full-desktop bbox path to 10.0 ms for direct-region capture. This measures capture cost, not game FPS; the update has not yet been measured during a match. Regression tests also simulate slow recognition and verify the full cooldown on success, pause and error paths.

Champion-select fields were checked against the running client's own API schema. Automated checks cover local-player matching, champion previews/swaps, spell changes, stale result rejection, and phase transitions. A real Tk panel was exercised with simulated champion-select states and real cached spell statistics; the local client connection was also verified. A live champion-select screen has not yet been used to verify this version end to end.

The available-champion view was checked on the portrait monitor with live-source rankings, portraits and spell pairs, including changes to the choice list, current-champion swaps, partial availability and match-start hiding. Ranking values were compared with ARAMKit's published champion page. Tests cover initial-card and bench eligibility, malformed/absent lists, stale asynchronous replies, coherent source caching and background request coalescing. The local endpoint schemas and current client selection logic were checked; a live selection session remains the final end-to-end verification.

The earlier-choice update was also checked with a simulated planning-phase session before any current champion or catalog had arrived. All three choices, rates and portraits appeared on the portrait monitor, and later catalog arrival preserved the rates. A fresh local-client poll used the known installation directly (36 ms in a read-only in-game check, versus 1,565 ms with the prior discovery path). Thread tests hold public requests open while verifying that local selection updates and ranking replies continue. These checks measure the corrected paths; live selection-to-display latency remains unmeasured.

Build statistics were checked against live ARAMKit champion data. The native Tk panel was exercised with real core sets and simulated champion-select, live-play, augment-open and augment-dismissed events. Tests cover stale build responses, missing data, coherent snapshot fallback, manual tab ownership and reroll/rescan stability. The new automatic Builds handoff has not yet been verified during a live match.

The follow-up panel was verified on the portrait monitor using real cached Brand statistics and simulated purchases/sales from two to three to four completed items and back. Icons, purchase-order selection, transformed-item matching, missing inventory, owned-item exclusion, and returning from Augments are covered by native or automated checks. The new inventory-driven switch has not yet been verified in a live match.

TFT source checks used current published data: 36 composition groups, 74 units and 249 augment references, plus exact three-item builds fetched for selected units. All three native views and their icons were visually checked on the portrait monitor with real source data. Simulated TFT-to-ARAM transitions, match-end hiding, manual/Auto visibility, conflicting mode data, duplicate names, stale replies and cached fallback are tested. TFT mode/queue identifiers were checked against the running client's queue metadata and Riot's queue catalog. A full live TFT match has not yet been used for end-to-end verification.

Arena mode identifiers were verified against Riot's queue catalog and the local client's queue metadata. Its title crop was checked against Riot's current Arena UI layout, and Windows OCR recognized a synthetic three-card image at those coordinates. Native champion, item and offer views were checked on the portrait monitor using current Brand statistics and icons, including a missing augment rate, Arena/TFT/ARAM transitions and match-end hiding. Automated tests cover separate sources, missing observations, background downloads, mode/champion transitions, stale responses, manual browsing, refreshes and icon loading. No live Arena match has yet been used for end-to-end validation.
